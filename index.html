01 / The problem

Annual plans
die on contact.

The target is shared; the model is fragmented. Finance sets the revenue target. Sales translates it into bookings. Marketing rebuilds pipeline requirements. Customer teams model retention and expansion. RevOps is left reconciling different definitions, timeframes, and assumptions after the decisions have already started moving. Plans built that way fail in the same five ways.

  1. 01

    Incomplete. A top-down number is pushed down the org before anyone has verified it bottom-up against real capacity.

  2. 02

    Misaligned. By month two, each function is quietly working from its own version of the plan.

  3. 03

    Stale. Built once and rarely revisited—not built to flex as the year actually unfolds.

  4. 04

    Untracked. Targets and initiatives exist on paper, but nobody is watching whether the plan is being executed.

  5. 05

    Slow to report. A holistic read for leadership takes days of manual reconciliation, not minutes.

03 / The rhythm

A plan that keeps time,
not one that flatlines.

Most plans are built once and then quietly drift from the year they were meant to describe. Drumbeat re-runs the plan against actuals every month: what held, what moved, and what has to change now. The model stays the same; the numbers catch up.

Because every function works from one connected model, a holistic read for leadership is a request rather than a reconciliation project—the same plan everyone has been operating from, current as of this month.

04 / Built for RevOps

A revenue plan should be
operable, not ornamental.

Traceable

Every assumption keeps its source.

Distinguish measured operating data from team judgment and temporary planning defaults. Review where an input came from before it drives a commitment.

Consistent

One assumption means the same thing everywhere.

Confirmed definitions carry through every view of the plan, so teams can review the result without debating which version or formula produced it.

Reconciled

Top-down ambition meets bottom-up capacity.

Keep the revenue requirement and the operating plan in the same view. When they disagree, the gap remains explicit instead of disappearing into a spreadsheet adjustment.

Accountable

Assumptions become owned decisions.

Give RevOps a reviewable record of what was confirmed, what still needs evidence, and where leadership intervention is required.
