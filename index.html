Annual planning, being rebuilt

The hand-off is where plans die.

Drumbeat is being built to carry a growth goal all the way down, as one model instead of five spreadsheets.

Get in touch ↗ See how it behaves

The problem

The strategy is sound. The hand-off is where it dies.

Growth strategy is built with the board over months. It reaches the people who have to land it with weeks to go — and each function plans its own part in its own spreadsheet, none of them interlocking.

What it is for

One goal, carried the whole way down.

Nobody lacks the judgment. What is missing is a model that holds all of it at once, and keeps holding it after the year starts.

Step one

Cascade the goal

From a growth number down to the segments, the people and the initiatives that have to produce it.

Step two

Test whether it is achievable

Against how long hiring actually takes, how long ramping actually takes, and what actually churns.

Step three

Keep everyone on one plan

So the version each function is working from is the same version, still, in March.

A different approach

Four places this works the other way round.

Not a longer feature list. The same four decisions, made differently.

Usually

Each function plans in its own spreadsheet, and none of them reconcile.

Here

One model everybody plans in, so the interlock is structural rather than a meeting.

Usually

A number appears in a cell and you are expected to trust it.

Here

Every number says where it came from — measured, asserted by you, or a benchmark standing in.

Usually

Top-down and bottom-up disagree, and the difference is quietly spread around until it disappears.

Here

The gap stays on screen and stays owned. Closing it is a decision somebody records, not a rounding.

Usually

You do not know something, so a plausible figure goes in and nobody remembers it was a guess.

Here

Say you do not know. It is marked unmeasured, and it stays marked until somebody measures it.

How it behaves

The parts you would be right to be sceptical about.

Planning software earns trust by showing its work, not by asserting it.

Deterministic

The numbers do not drift.

The numbers are deterministic. A language model helps you map and describe your data. It never produces a number.

Confirmed, not assumed

Nothing is filled in behind you.

Nothing becomes a number without you confirming it. If you do not know something, say so — it is marked unmeasured rather than quietly guessed.

Gaps stay visible

Disagreement is shown, not smoothed.

Where the top-down and the bottom-up disagree, the difference is shown and owned — never spread across the levers until it disappears.

Provenance

Every number says where it came from.

Measured from your data, asserted by you, or a benchmark standing in — and which one it is never stops being visible.

Plan the year from measured facts, and nothing pretending to be either.
